\subsection{Whole-family queries on fixed views}
The retrospective family study reuses the 48 main worlds and the stored threshold for $.001$. Its 24 comparison cells are three source constructions (OBS, projected R, projected Fusion) $\times$ two R sample budgets $\times$ two radii $\times$ finite/population information. Each cell retains 48 worlds $\times$ five repetitions, giving 5,760 comparison positions, not independent datasets. The underlying 2,880 finite and 288 population program records reduce to 2,160 distinct exact $(\C,w,\pi_0)$ requests. Both solvers run each request: 4,320 synthetic calls. Deduplication saves computation without dropping comparison positions.

We decode saved inputs exactly, normalize policy probabilities rationally and recheck each position's three saved duals. Among 240 primary finite-Fusion cases, 12 already have checked policy gains; 121 have checked witnesses constant across actions within each context, with $F(m)=0$, constructed before complete family optimization. The remaining 107 are the fixed recovery set. This family-level uncertainty differs from a fixed policy lacking a positive lower bound. Theorem~\ref{thm:simultaneous} permits selection on the inference view; this retrospective analysis is not a new confirmatory or out-of-sample learning test.
